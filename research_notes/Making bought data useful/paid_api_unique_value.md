### Inferences
- The stored `inputs` cannot be trusted for any `guessed: true` row. An agent that fills `{"query": "..."}` into a Coresignal filter body, or a phone number into Serper places, will either get a 4xx or pay for an empty or irrelevant answer. The app should keep a hand-curated parameter template per endpoint (the "good query" examples below) and treat the AI-tagged schema only as a discovery hint.
- Price ladder per call: Quicknode $0.0001 < MrScraper $0.0003–0.001 < Exa contents $0.001 < BlockRun nano $0.001–0.005 < Serper $0.002–0.006 < Exa search $0.007 < Tomba $0.01 < Coresignal search $0.021 < PredictLeads $0.04 ≈ Coresignal collect $0.042 < Coresignal multi-source $0.084. A full "company dossier" (Coresignal multi-source + PredictLeads financing + news + jobs) costs about $0.20.

## Q1. Coresignal: what proprietary or structured data does it have that Google does not, and how do you query it well?

### Takeaway
Coresignal's real edge is **history and aggregation**: monthly headcount series (3-year rolling window), headcount by country/department-type breakdowns, executive arrivals and departures, job-posting count deltas, plus person-level work histories and a 500M+ job-postings archive. Google shows today's LinkedIn headline number at best. It never shows a time series or a filterable list. A single-company "what is X" lookup is a waste. The value is in `*_by_month` and `*_change` fields and in filter searches that return lists Google cannot build.

### Cited Findings
- Coverage claims: 70M+ companies with 500+ fields, collected since 2016 ([Coresignal Company Dataset](https://coresignal.com/company-dataset/)). Third parties report 72M–75M+ ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/crm-and-sales-tools/coresignal-review/), [Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/reviews/coresignal-review)).
- Employee data: 921M+ records with 300+ fields and history back to 2016, per the homepage ([coresignal.com](https://coresignal.com/)). Other pages cite 839M–907M ([Datamagnet](https://www.datamagnet.co/post/coresignal-vs-crustdata/)). Fields cover experience, education, skills and salary ([Coresignal blog](https://coresignal.com/blog/coresignal-new-multi-source-dataset/)).
- Job postings: 580M+ with 85+ fields and data since 2020, per the homepage ([coresignal.com](https://coresignal.com/)). Datarade says 486M+ records, 80+ fields, 6+ years ([Datarade](https://datarade.ai/data-products/job-postings-data-coresignal)). The numbers conflict by page and date.
- Company records keep a stable ID across deliveries and carry historical fields such as headcount and hiring activity ([Coresignal Company Dataset](https://coresignal.com/company-dataset/)).
- `employees_count_by_month` is one of the `*_by_month` breakdowns put on a consistent **three-year rolling window** (May 2025 release) ([release notes May 2025](https://docs.coresignal.com/release-notes/may-2025)). Duplicate values in it were fixed in February 2025 ([release notes Feb 2025](https://docs.coresignal.com/release-notes/february-2025)).
- The sibling field `employees_count_inferred_by_month` is an array like `[{"employees_count_inferred": 20, "date": "202504"}]`, with dates as YYYYMM strings ([Coresignal multi-source company dictionary](https://docs.coresignal.com/company-data/multi-source-company-data/dictionary-multi-source-company-data), via search summary).
- December 2025 breaking change: several `*_by_month` fields, including `employees_count_by_country_by_month`, changed from flattened to nested ([release notes Dec 2025](https://docs.coresignal.com/release-notes/december-2025)).
- `key_executive_arrivals` is "a list of new executives in the company. Executives are considered employees that have a decision_maker = true flag". A later version adds `arrival_date`. In April 2025 the executive identifier was renamed from `member_id` to `parent_id` ([Multi-source Company API dictionary](https://docs.coresignal.com/company-api/multi-source-company-api/data-dictionary-multi-source-company-api); [April 2025 notes](https://docs.coresignal.com/release-notes/april-2025)).
- `active_job_postings_count_change` is an object with current count plus monthly, quarterly and yearly changes and percentages. Sample: `"active_job_postings_count_change": { "current": 11540, "change_monthly": 54 ...}` ([Multi-source Company dictionary](https://docs.coresignal.com/company-data/multi-source-company-data/dictionary-multi-source-company-data); [sample](https://docs.coresignal.com/company-api/multi-source-company-api/sample-multi-source-company-api-data)).
- Base Company API simple search filters include `industry`, `country` and `employees_count_gte`, and values can be OR-combined. `POST /search/filter` handles simple filters (industry, country, headcount range, founding year). `POST /search/es_dsl` takes Elasticsearch query language and supports sorting by `employees_count` ([Search Filters: Base Company API](https://docs.coresignal.com/company-api/base-company-api/endpoints/search-filters); [Elasticsearch DSL](https://docs.coresignal.com/company-api/base-company-api/endpoints/elasticsearch-dsl); [API Evangelist](https://apievangelist.com/2026/11/04/find-target-companies-and-enrich-your-crm-with-firmographic-data-from-coresignal/)).
- Base Jobs API search filters include `created_at_gte`, `application_active`, `country` (the country the employer hires in, parsed in-house) and `industry` ([Search Filters: Base Jobs API](https://docs.coresignal.com/jobs-api/base-jobs-api/endpoints/search-filters)).
- Refresh: daily, weekly or monthly depending on source, with a real-time API option for employee data ([Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/reviews/coresignal-review)). Company API webhooks notify on changes ([Coresignal Company Dataset](https://coresignal.com/company-dataset/)).
- Search/collect pattern: search returns IDs, then collect fetches full records ([Coresignal quickstart](https://docs.coresignal.com/guides/quickstart-guide); [apis.io search-collect workflow](https://apis.io/arazzos/coresignal/coresignal-company-search-collect-workflow/)). This matches the catalog's $0.021 search vs $0.042/$0.084 collect pricing.

### Inferences
- **Unique vs Google.** Google can tell you "Acme has ~500 employees on LinkedIn" today. It cannot give you (1) the 36-month headcount curve, (2) the curve split by country, (3) which execs joined or left and when, (4) how many job postings are open now vs last quarter, or (5) a filtered list such as "US fintechs with ≥50 staff". All five are what you pay for.
- **Pick the tier by need.** `company_base`/`company_clean` ($0.042) is enough for firmographics. Pay $0.084 for `company_multi_source` only when you will actually use the `*_by_month`, `key_executive_*` or `*_change` fields. The time-series and exec-change fields cited above are documented on the multi-source dictionary pages. Whether base/clean carry them was not verified.
- **Poor query.** `company_base/search/filter` with `{"query":"fintech startups"}`. This is the guessed catalog shape and not a documented filter, so expect an error or a blind result. Another poor query: calling `company_multi_source/enrich?domain=stripe.com` just to read the description and employee count, which Google or Tomba gives for $0.01.
- **Good query, step 1 (list).** `POST company_base/search/filter` with a body such as `{"industry":"Financial Services","country":"(United States OR Canada)","employees_count_gte":50}`. This returns IDs for $0.021. Field names are from the cited filter docs. The exact OR syntax and the founded-year field names should be checked against the docs.
- **Good query, step 2 (depth on the shortlist).** `GET company_multi_source/enrich?domain=acme.com` ($0.084). Then compute: headcount change over 6/12 months from `employees_count_by_month`; whether `key_executive_departures` (assumed counterpart, unverified) outnumber arrivals over the last 12 months; and `active_job_postings_count_change.change_quarterly`. Return derived numbers ("headcount −18% YoY, hiring down 40% QoQ, CFO left in March") rather than the raw blob.
- **Good job-posting query.** `POST job_base/search/filter` with `{"title":"machine learning engineer","country":"Germany","application_active":true,"created_at_gte":"2026-10-01 00:00:00"}` gives a live, dated list of postings that Google Jobs shows only partially and without a stable archive. The date string format is unverified.
- **People.** `employee_base/search/filter` and `collect` give work histories, useful for "where did this company's engineers go" or "how long do people stay". This is sensitive personal data. The app should limit it to aggregate questions.

### Gaps
- Exact names of the funding fields (e.g. `last_funding_round_*`), revenue estimate fields, web-traffic fields, review fields and `key_executive_departures` in multi-source data could not be confirmed. The dictionary page was unreachable and the search summaries did not show them.
- Whether Orthogonal exposes `es_dsl` (the catalog lists only `search/filter` and `search/filter/preview`), and whether the preview response includes enough fields to skip collect.
- Real-world accuracy of headcount curves for small (<20 staff) companies. No independent accuracy study was found.

## Q2. PredictLeads: what does it have that Google does not, and how do you query it well?

### Takeaway
PredictLeads turns scattered news and careers pages into **dated, categorized event rows**: funding rounds with a normalized USD amount, news events in 30–46 fixed categories (acquires, expands_offices_to, decreases_headcount_by, hires, launches…), job openings with first/last-seen dates and O*NET categories, plus tech-stack detections, products and GitHub repos. Google can find a single headline. It cannot give you "every funding event for this domain with amount_normalized and date" or "all companies that announced layoffs since 1 Oct". The `discover/*` endpoints and the `first_seen_at_from` date filter are where the value is.

### Cited Findings
- Endpoints: per-company lists at `/api/v3/companies/{domain}/news_events`, `/financing_events`, `/job_openings`, plus single-record lookups by ID ([docs.predictleads.com/v3](https://docs.predictleads.com/v3), via search summary).
- Job openings fields: title, URL, first and last seen dates, location, category, seniority, description, salary, contract type, and O*NET job category codes. Postings come from company websites and ATS integrations ([job openings dataset](https://docs.predictleads.com/v3/data_models/job_openings_dataset)).
- Financing events cover venture rounds, private equity, grants and loans. `financing_type` holds a value like "Series E funding". `amount` is the raw string, `amount_normalized` is an integer in USD, and `source_urls` lists the news articles. Investors and investment date are also in the model ([PredictLeads API endpoints](https://docs.predictleads.com/v3/api_endpoints); [financing_type attribute](https://docs.predictleads.com/guide/financing_events_dataset/data_model/financing_events_dataset/data/financing_event/attributes/financing_type); [PredictLeads blog, May 2026](https://blog.predictleads.com/2026/05/05/using-predictleads-financing-events-dataset-for-investor-research-workflows)).
- The financing dataset is extracted from the news events data and covers mostly startup rounds ([docs.predictleads.com](https://docs.predictleads.com/)).
- The company-level financing endpoint takes `page`, `limit`, `first_seen_at_from`, `first_seen_at_until`. The per-company path costs $0.04 per call ([Glasser listing](https://glasser.ai/data-sources/predictleads); [x402 bazaar listing](https://opensea.io/tools/x402-bazaar/base/2659358751584464742)).
- News event categories: `acquires` (acquisition group: "Company acquired another company"), `expands_offices_to` (expansion: "Company opens new offices in another town, state, country or continent"), `decreases_headcount_by` (cost_cutting: "Company lays off employees"). `hires` (leadership) has 1,272,025 events and `launches` (new offering, with `integrates_with`, `is_developing`) has 3,432,419 events ([PredictLeads news event categories guide](https://predictleads.com/blog/predictleads-news-event-categories-guide/); [37 categories blog](https://predictleads.com/blog/the-37-news-event-categories-that-signal-a-gtm-opportunity/)).
- The category count conflicts: 46 event types on one PredictLeads page, 37 on another blog, 28 in a directory, 29 per Explorium ([PredictLeads news_events page](https://predictleads.com/news_events); [37-categories blog](https://predictleads.com/blog/the-37-news-event-categories-that-signal-a-gtm-opportunity/); [Explorium comparison](https://www.explorium.ai/compare/predictleads/)).
- The Connections dataset has Connection Category, Source Category, Source URL, Context, First Seen At, Last Seen At ([connections source_url](https://docs.predictleads.com/v3/data_models/connections_dataset/data/connection/attributes/source_url)).

### Inferences
- **Unique vs Google.** (1) Normalized funding amounts and round types in one list per company, with source URLs to verify. (2) Event categories you can filter on: Google News returns articles, PredictLeads returns "this is a `decreases_headcount_by` event dated X". (3) Job openings with `first_seen`/`last_seen`. A posting that stays open for months, or a sudden wave of new ones, is a signal, and Google Jobs shows neither the age nor the history. (4) Technology detections with first-seen dates, e.g. "started using Snowflake in 2025". (5) `discover/*` queries across all companies, which Google cannot do.
- **Poor query.** `GET companies/stripe.com/news_events` with no date bound and default limit. You pay $0.04 for a page of old, mixed events and the agent has to sift them. Another poor query: `companies/{domain}` alone just to get name, location and description, which a $0.01 Tomba call or a free Google result gives.
- **Good query, single company.** `GET companies/acme.com/financing_events` (last raise date, `amount_normalized`, `financing_type`, investors). Then `GET companies/acme.com/news_events?first_seen_at_from=2026-05-01` for the last six months, keeping only categories like `decreases_headcount_by`, `hires`, `acquires`, `launches`. The `first_seen_at_from` name is cited for financing. That it works on news_events, and the category filter's parameter name, are unverified.
- **Good query, discovery.** `GET discover/financing_events?first_seen_at_from=2026-10-01&limit=100` for "who raised in the last 7 weeks". `GET discover/news_events` filtered to `decreases_headcount_by` for "who announced layoffs". Each is one $0.04 call that replaces dozens of Google searches.
- **Good query, hiring signal.** `GET companies/acme.com/job_openings` and compute: count of postings first seen in the last 30 days, share by O*NET category (e.g. sales vs engineering), and postings open longer than 90 days.

### Gaps
- The authoritative list of category slugs and the exact filter parameter names for `discover/news_events`, `discover/job_openings` and `discover/companies` (docs unreachable).
- Coverage numbers (companies tracked, events per year) and refresh cadence were not found in the search summaries.
- Fields of `technology_detections` (first_seen_at/last_seen_at assumed by analogy) and `github_repositories` (stars over time?) were not confirmed.

## Q3. Exa: what does it have that Google does not, and how do you query it well?

### Takeaway
Exa is not a proprietary database like Coresignal or PredictLeads. It is a different **search engine** (embeddings-first), with a 50M+ company-page index and a 1B+ people index, date filters on published date, domain allow/deny lists, and built-in page contents, highlights and LLM summaries (including schema-shaped JSON) in the same call. Its value over Google is (a) finding things by description rather than keywords ("startups like X", "papers that argue Y"), and (b) returning clean, cited text an agent can use without a second scrape. For plain fact lookups ("who is the CEO of X"), Google or Serper at $0.002 is as good and cheaper.

### Cited Findings
- Categories: company, research paper, news, personal site, financial report, people. The company and people indexes hold 50M+ company pages with metadata and 1B+ people with metadata (job, education) ([Exa search reference](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/search.md); [Exa company vertical](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/verticals/company)).
- For the people category, `includeDomains` only accepts LinkedIn domains ([Exa search reference](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/search.md)). A community guide says people searches support no date or text filters ([Exa search API guide mirror](https://the-obstacle-is-the-way.github.io/erdos-banger/vendor-docs/exa/exa-api-reference)).
- The current docs integrate news into the main search with no category needed. Older guides still show `category: "news"` ([Exa news vertical](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/verticals/news-for-coding-agents.md)).
- Exa's best-practices page says to use `startPublishedDate` and `endPublishedDate`, because `startCrawlDate`/`endCrawlDate` are ignored. Use `contents.maxAgeHours: 0` instead of `livecrawl: "always"`, and don't send both ([Exa search best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/search/best-practices)). A third-party reference still lists `livecrawl` values never/fallback/preferred/always, so docs may be out of sync ([Kalshi research platform vendor docs](https://the-obstacle-is-the-way.github.io/kalshi-starter-code-python/_vendor-docs/exa-api-reference/)).
- Highlights go under `contents`. Exa sizes them per result, and you set `highlights.maxCharacters` only for a fixed limit ([Exa best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/search/best-practices)). `summary` takes `query` and `schema` for structured output ([Novita Exa reference](https://docs.novita.ai/api-reference/model-apis-exa-search); [Kalshi vendor docs](https://the-obstacle-is-the-way.github.io/kalshi-starter-code-python/_vendor-docs/exa-api-reference/)).
- Text and highlights carry no extra charge. Summary costs extra per result because it runs a model on each page ([Exa quickstart](https://exa.ai/docs/search/quickstart)).
- Third-party pricing: instant ~$4/1k, auto/fast ~$7/1k, deep $12–15/1k, with up to 10 results included ([KeiroLabs Exa guide 2026](https://keirolabs.cloud/blog/exa-ai-api-guide-2026)). This matches the catalog's $0.007 per search. These rates are not from Exa's pricing page.
- `findSimilar` takes a URL instead of a query and returns similar pages ([Exa best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/search/best-practices)). It is **not** in the app's catalog (only `/search` and `/contents` are listed).

### Inferences
- **Poor query.** `{"query":"AI startups"}` with default type and no contents. That is a keyword-style query to a neural engine, returns 10 bare links, and the agent must pay again (`/contents`) or scrape to read them. Another poor query: `{"query":"Stripe revenue 2025"}`, a fact lookup Google/Serper does better for $0.002.
- **Good query, discovery by description.** `POST /search` with `{"query":"seed-stage startup building payroll software for construction contractors","category":"company","numResults":25,"contents":{"highlights":{"query":"funding customers founded"},"summary":{"query":"What does this company sell and to whom?"}}}`. Write the query as a description of the page you want, not as keywords.
- **Good query, fresh news on a company.** `{"query":"Acme Corp layoffs or restructuring","startPublishedDate":"2026-09-01T00:00:00Z","excludeDomains":["acme.com"],"numResults":10,"contents":{"highlights":true,"maxAgeHours":24}}`. Excluding the company's own domain removes press-release spin.
- **Good query, structured extraction.** Add `"summary":{"schema":{...}}` (e.g. `{"price_usd":"number","plan_names":"string[]"}`) to get JSON per result. Use it only on a short result list, because summary is billed per result.
- **`/contents` at $0.001** is the cheapest way to turn a known URL into clean text. It competes with MrScraper `scrape-markdown` ($0.001). Exa's is cached and fast; MrScraper renders JS and uses proxies (`super`, `proxyCountry`).

### Gaps
- Exact current `type` values (auto/fast/instant/deep/neural/keyword) and whether the x402 listing at $0.007 covers deep search or only auto/fast.
- Whether `summary` and `maxAgeHours` live crawls are billed extra through the x402 listing, or included in $0.007.
- The structured fields the company category returns (headcount? funding?). The company vertical page was found but not readable.

## Q4. Serper, Tomba, MrScraper, BlockRun, Quicknode/Chainlink (already used by the app): do they add anything over Google?

### Takeaway
**Serper is Google**, so it gives a person nothing they can't see themselves. Its value is machine access: structured JSON, date filters (`tbs`), geo (`gl`), and verticals like reviews, places, patents and scholar with fields such as ratings, review dates and citation counts, all for $0.002. **Tomba** adds a non-Google asset in its email/people graph and phone carrier validation, but its company-by-domain record overlaps with free sources. **MrScraper** adds rendering and proxy reach, not data. **BlockRun prediction-market data** and **Chainlink-via-Quicknode** add live numeric feeds that Google shows only as stale snippets. They are the clearest "can't Google it" cases for the trading agents, though the raw sources are also free directly.

### Cited Findings
- Serper parameters: `gl` (ISO 3166-1 country), `hl` (ISO 639-1 language), `num` (1–100, default 10), `page`, `autocorrect`, and `tbs` date filter with values `qdr:h`, `qdr:d`, `qdr:w`, `qdr:m`, `qdr:y`. No Serper-specific source confirmed custom date ranges ([apis.io Serper schema](https://apis.io/schemas/serper/serper-search-request/); [LiteLLM Serper docs](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/search/serper.md); [LangChain SerperParameters](https://reference.langchain.com/javascript/langchain-community/tools/serper/SerperParameters)).
- Serper reviews take `placeId` (also `fid`/`cid` depending on version) and `sortBy`: `mostRelevant` (default), `newest`, `highestRating`, `lowestRating`. This comes from an MCP wrapper, not Serper docs ([garylab serper-mcp-server](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/garylab/serper-mcp-server/tools/google_search_reviews)). SerpApi uses different names (`sort_by`, `data_id`) that won't work on Serper ([SerpApi Google Maps Reviews API](https://serpapi.com/google-maps-reviews-api)).
- Catalog output descriptions for Serper places/reviews/patents/scholar/shopping, Tomba, MrScraper and BlockRun are quoted in Q0 above. — `.data/agento.db`
- The app already sells Chainlink reads (BTC, ETH, SOL, LINK, AVAX, BNB, USDC, gold, silver, EUR, GBP, JPY), gas on five chains, Bitcoin fees and USDC balances via Quicknode at 0.0001 USDC ([README.md line 280](/home/user/agento-ubc/README.md)). Freshness is enforced, e.g. "a Chainlink price over 25 hours old" fails ([README.md line 180](/home/user/agento-ubc/README.md)).

### Inferences
- **Serper.** Poor: `/serper/search {"q":"Acme"}`, which repeats what the person sees on Google. Good: `/serper/news {"q":"\"Acme Corp\" layoffs","tbs":"qdr:m","gl":"us","num":20}` for a dated, past-month list. `/serper/places {"q":"dentist","location":"Austin, Texas"}` gets `cid`/placeId, then `/serper/reviews {"placeId":"…","sortBy":"newest"}` gives a dated review stream. Compute "rating of last 50 reviews vs lifetime rating", a trend Google's star average hides. `/serper/patents` and `/serper/scholar` give assignee/filing dates and citation counts in structured form. Verdict: worth it for automation and computed trends. Never present raw Serper output to a user as "paid insight".
- **Tomba.** `companies/find?domain=` ($0.01) is mostly Google-equivalent firmographics. The non-Google pieces are `email-count` (department/seniority breakdown as a cheap size proxy), `location` (where employees are), `phone-validator` (carrier/line type), and `email-verifier`. Use it as a cheap first pass and as a verifier, not as an insight source.
- **MrScraper.** It adds reach, not data: JS rendering (`browserRendering`), anti-bot (`super`), geo proxies (`proxyCountry`), and `scrape-json` with a `prompt` for structured extraction. Good use: pull a pricing page or careers page into JSON so it can be diffed over time. The insight comes from repeated snapshots (a watch), not a single scrape.
- **BlockRun.** Live odds plus the filters Google lacks (`min_volume_7d`, `min_open_interest`, `end_before`), and especially `smart-money`, `top-holders` and `candlesticks` history. Poor: `pm/polymarket/markets?search=election`. Good: `pm/polymarket/markets?search=fed+rate&status=active&min_volume_7d=50000&end_before=2027-01-01`, then `candlesticks/{hash}` for the 30-day path and `market/{hash}/smart-money` for positioning. Caveat from general knowledge, not verified this session: Polymarket's and Kalshi's own market-data APIs are publicly readable for free, so BlockRun's value is convenience plus derived wallet analytics (smart money, cohorts).
- **Chainlink via Quicknode.** It is not a data advantage over a price website for a human. It is a **verifiable second source** that an agent can cite on-chain before trading, at $0.0001. Keep it, framed as "checked against Chainlink", not "exclusive data".

### Gaps
- Serper's own docs (serper.dev) were not retrieved. Parameter names come from third-party wrappers.
- Tomba docs were not retrieved. Field names for phone-validator output (carrier, line type, risk) are from catalog descriptions only.
- The fields BlockRun's `smart-money` endpoint returns, and how "smart" wallets are defined.

## Q5. Which combinations across providers produce an insight none gives alone?

### Takeaway
Single paid calls mostly give a fact. The "can't Google it" insight comes from **joining a time series with a dated event**: headcount or hiring trend from Coresignal against the last funding round from PredictLeads, layoff and exec-departure events against job-posting deltas, review trends against hiring trends. Each combination below costs $0.10–0.25 and answers a question no single source, Google included, answers.

### Cited Findings
- Coresignal provides monthly headcount (`employees_count_by_month`, 3-year window), executive arrivals with dates, and job-posting count changes ([Coresignal release notes May 2025](https://docs.coresignal.com/release-notes/may-2025); [Multi-source Company API dictionary](https://docs.coresignal.com/company-api/multi-source-company-api/data-dictionary-multi-source-company-api)).
- PredictLeads provides funding events with `amount_normalized` and type, categorized news events (`decreases_headcount_by`, `hires`, `expands_offices_to`, `acquires`, `launches`), and job openings with first/last-seen dates ([PredictLeads API endpoints](https://docs.predictleads.com/v3/api_endpoints); [categories guide](https://predictleads.com/blog/predictleads-news-event-categories-guide/); [job openings dataset](https://docs.predictleads.com/v3/data_models/job_openings_dataset)).
- Exa provides date-bounded neural news/company search with highlights and schema summaries ([Exa best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/search/best-practices)). Serper provides dated reviews with `sortBy: newest` ([serper-mcp-server reviews tool](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/garylab/serper-mcp-server/tools/google_search_reviews)).

### Inferences
Combination recipes (endpoint → derived signal → insight):
1. **Runway risk** (≈$0.12). PredictLeads `companies/{d}/financing_events` gives last raise date and amount. Coresignal `company_multi_source/enrich?domain={d}` gives headcount at the raise month vs now from `employees_count_by_month`. Insight: "Raised $12M Series A 18 months ago; headcount down 20% since; open jobs down 60% QoQ" = likely runway stress. The reverse (headcount +40% since raise) = burning fast toward a next round.
2. **Real vs announced layoffs** (≈$0.12). PredictLeads `news_events` (`decreases_headcount_by` in the last 6 months) plus Coresignal headcount curve. Insight: whether the announced cut shows up in the data, and whether rehiring has started (`active_job_postings_count_change.change_monthly` > 0).
3. **Strategy shift** (≈$0.10). PredictLeads `job_openings` by O*NET category (sales vs engineering share, first seen in the last 60 days), `technology_detections` (new tools first seen), and `news_events` `launches`/`integrates_with`. Insight: "pivoting from product build to go-to-market", or "moving into data infrastructure".
4. **Leadership instability** (≈$0.13). Coresignal `key_executive_arrivals`/departures, plus PredictLeads `hires` events, plus Exa news (`startPublishedDate` = last 90 days, `excludeDomains` = own domain, highlights). Insight: "3 C-level changes in 9 months, two not announced in the press".
5. **Customer sentiment vs execution** (≈$0.03, local businesses or consumer brands). Serper `places` → `reviews {sortBy:"newest"}` for the recent-vs-lifetime rating, plus PredictLeads job openings or Coresignal headcount. Insight: "ratings slipping while staff shrinks" (a service-quality problem), as opposed to a one-off bad review.
6. **Lead list with timing** (≈$0.10 + $0.04/company). PredictLeads `discover/financing_events` (raised in the last 60 days), then Coresignal `company_base/search/filter` or enrich for size/industry fit, then Tomba `email-count`/`email-finder` for reach. Insight: "fresh-funded, right-sized, hiring for the role you sell to", a list Google cannot produce.
7. **Prediction-market sanity check** (≈$0.01). BlockRun `markets` + `candlesticks` + `smart-money`, Exa news in the last 48h (`maxAgeHours`), and Chainlink price for crypto-linked markets. Insight: "odds moved 15 points without news; smart wallets on the other side", which is a reason to wait.

Query-design rules that make the bought answer useful:
- Always bound by date (`first_seen_at_from`, `startPublishedDate`, `tbs`, `created_at_gte`). Undated pulls pay for stale rows.
- Buy lists cheaply (search/preview/discover at $0.021–0.04) and buy depth (collect/multi-source at $0.084) only for the shortlist.
- Return **derived numbers with dates and a source URL** ("−20% since Mar 2025 raise, per Coresignal; raise per PredictLeads, source: TechCrunch link"), never raw JSON or a summary Google could have written.
- Show what Google would have said next to what the paid data adds. That directly answers "why pay?".

### Gaps
- None of these combinations was run (no paid calls allowed), so hit rates (how often PredictLeads has a financing event for a typical SMB, and how complete Coresignal's monthly series is for <50-staff companies) are unknown. A small, owner-approved test spend on 5–10 known companies would settle it.
- The domain-matching reliability between Coresignal and PredictLeads (same company, different domains or redirects) is unknown.

## Q6. Verdict: which purchases are worth paying for versus Google?

### Takeaway
Worth paying: Coresignal multi-source (time series), PredictLeads (dated, categorized events and discover lists), and BlockRun analytics, each when the query asks for **change over time or a filtered list**. Conditionally worth it: Exa (description-based discovery plus clean contents) and Serper (only as cheap automation of Google, never sold as insight). Low unique value: Tomba company lookup, MrScraper (a tool, not data), Chainlink (a trust anchor, not information).
